# Offensive Line Training Program

A four-day, high-volume, high-intensity off-season lifting plan engineered for offensive line mechanics (ages 14–25). This program couples what wins in the trenches—horizontal force for drive blocks, angle-specific stiffness for pass-pro anchors, rapid, accurate hand strikes, and trunk/bracing under load—with methods supported by top sport-science and NSCA guidance.

## Why These Methods Map to O-Line Play (Science in Brief)

1. **Plays are short and violent, rest is relatively long** → Train alactic power and repeatability, not roadwork. Typical play lasts only a few seconds with ~25–35 s between snaps in college/NFL datasets; long-distance running is counter-specific for linemen per NSCA.

2. **Drive blocking = horizontal force** → Very-heavy sled work (loads ~70–96% body mass depending on surface) maximizes horizontal power; even ~80% BW improves first-step acceleration.

3. **Pass protection = anchor & posture** → Isometric training is joint-angle specific (with some carryover), and longer-length isometrics stiffen tendons—useful for "hold your ground" at the joint angles you actually anchor in.

4. **Front-loaded squatting** keeps the torso upright (OL posture) with comparable lower-body stimulus to back squat and lower knee/lumbar moments.

5. **Strongman carries** (yoke/farmer's) build trunk stiffness under gait and load-carriage mechanics that transfer to maintaining base while moving.

6. **Nordic hamstrings** reduce hamstring injury risk while supporting sprint/accel qualities.

7. **Neck strength matters** in collision sports; higher neck strength is associated with lower concussion odds in high-school athletes.

8. **Power & strength loading rules** (place power first; heavy loads for max strength; moderate for hypertrophy; explosive intent always) come straight from NSCA.

## Weekly Split (Mon - Tue - Thu - Fri)

Each day lists **Advanced (17–25)** first, then **Developmental (14–16 or newer lifters)** adjustments beneath. Percentages are of current 1RM; use RPE 7–9 if 1RM unknown. Place power work first every session.

### MONDAY — DRIVE BLOCK FORCE (Lower-body max strength + horizontal force)

1. **Power Clean from Blocks** – 5×3 @ 70–85% (full recovery)
2. **Front Squat** (high-bar back squat acceptable) – 5×5 @ 75–85%
3. **Very-Heavy Sled Push** – 8–10×10–15 m @ 70–90% BW (push hard, full recovery 2–3 min)
4. **Barbell Hip Thrust** – 4×6 @ ~80%
5. **Nordic Hamstring** – 4×6 controlled
6. **Ankle/Calf Isometrics** – 3×30–45 s heavy hold (sled handles or pins)
7. **Stance→6×5 m first-step accelerations** (focus: shin angle, hips through)

**Developmental:** Power clean becomes Clean Pull 5×3 @ 80–90% of clean-pull 1RM; front squat 4×5 @ 70–80%; sled 6–8×10 m @ 50–70% BW as technique is built.

**Why it helps OL:** Front squats preserve upright trunk under load (OL posture). Heavy sled develops the exact horizontal force needed to move bodies off the ball.

### TUESDAY — PASS-PRO POWER & STRIKE (Upper-body power + anchor mechanics)

1. **Bench Press (cluster: 2+2+2)** – 6 clusters @ 82–87% (20–30 s intra-cluster; ~3 min between)
2. **Push Press** – 5×3 @ 70–80% (bar speed emphasis)
3. **Med-Ball Chest "Punches"** – 6×3 (heavy MB; maximal velocity)
4. **Isometric "Anchor" Half-Squat Holds** – 4×10–12 s against pins or belt-squat at ~110–120° knee angle; add band/chain to mimic bull-rush line of action
5. **Weighted Chin-ups or Chest-Supported Row** – 4×6–8 heavy
6. **Neck (flex/ext/lat)** – 3×12 each plane; **Grip** – crush + pinch 3×20–30 s

**Developmental:** Flat bench 5×5 @ 75–82%; Push-up throws or plyo push-ups 5×4; isometric holds bodyweight only to nail positions.

**Why it helps OL:** Clustered heavy pressing + med-ball punches develop rate of force in the "strike," while angle-specific isometrics build anchor stiffness at the exact joint angles you defend at. Neck work supports collision tolerance.

### THURSDAY — POWER & BASE UNDER LOAD (Lower-body speed + carries + lateral base)

1. **Trap-Bar Jumps** – 6×3 @ 20–40% 1RM (full reset every rep)
2. **Front-Squat Speed Sets** – 4×3 @ ~60–70% (bar speed ≥0.6–0.8 m/s if you track velocity)
3. **Romanian Deadlift** – 4×6 @ ~75–80%
4. **Yoke Walk** – 5×20 m heavy but clean (base width, brace, neutral torso)
   - No yoke? **Farmer's Carries** – 5×30–40 m heavy
5. **Backwards Sled Drags** – 6×20 m moderate-heavy
6. **Lateral Shuffle March (band-resisted)** – 3×15 m each side (hips low, feet under hips)

**Developmental:** Trap-bar jumps 6×3 bodyweight or light DB; RDL 3×8 @ ~70%; carries moderate loads with perfect posture.

**Why it helps OL:** Loaded carries/yoke walks train bracing and base while moving; sled drags and trap-bar jumps develop concentric force and stiffness relevant to short-space displacement.

### FRIDAY — UPPER MAX + "ARMOR" & GAME-SPECIFIC ENERGY

1. **Close-Grip Bench or Incline Bench** – 5×4–6 @ 80–88%
2. **One-Arm Landmine Press** – 4×6/side (transfer force through pillar)
3. **Heavy Single-Arm Row** – 4×8/side
4. **Anti-Rotation (Pallof / Cable Hold)** – 3×8–10/side (2–3 s holds)
5. **Neck + Grip** (variation not used Tues) – 3×12 / 3×20–30 s
6. **"Play clock" conditioning** (prowler/sled, battle ropes, or short hills):
   - 2–3 blocks of 8–12 reps: 5 s all-out work : 25–35 s rest, 3–4 min between blocks

**Why this conditioning matches football:** It mirrors the play:rest rhythm rather than steady-state running.

## 12-Week Progression (Template Repeats Weekly)

Use these % zones; keep explosive intent and crisp positions. (NSCA loading & power guidelines.)

### Weeks 1–4 (Accumulation – high volume, build work capacity/technique)

- **Main strength lifts:** 70–80% 1RM, 5×5 or 4×6
- **Power lifts:** 70–80%, 5×3
- **Sled push:** 60–80% BW, 8–10×10–15 m
- **Carries:** moderate-heavy, total 100–150 m
- **Total work sets/day:** ~22–28

### Weeks 5–8 (Intensification – max strength & specific force)

- **Strength lifts:** 82–90% 1RM, 5×3–4×4
- **Power lifts:** 75–85%, 5×2–3
- **Sled push:** 80–100% BW, 8–10×10 m (heavier, shorter)
- **Isometric anchors:** 4×10–12 s at pass-pro angles
- **Carries:** heavier, 100–140 m

### Weeks 9–12 (Power/Conversion – speed of force & peak horizontal mechanics)

- **Strength lifts:** 80–85% 1RM, 3–4×3 (quality, speed)
- **Power lifts:** 70–80%, 6×2–3 (fast bar)
- **Trap-bar jumps:** stay 20–40% 1RM
- **Sled push:** 70–96% BW depending on surface to hit your max-power load (see below), 6–8×10 m
- **Conditioning:** 3 blocks of 10–12 reps (5 s : 25–35 s)

## How to Set the Sled Load Precisely (Horizontal Force "Dial-In")

Two reliable options (use the best you can access):

1. **Max-Power method (Cross et al.):** Your power is maximized when sled load causes ~50% decrement in your unresisted sprint velocity; this typically falls around ~70–96% BW on common surfaces. Use that for most push reps in Weeks 9–12.

2. **Very-heavy method (Morin et al.):** If timing gates aren't available, work at ~80% BW for short 10–15 m pushes to drive horizontal force qualities.

## Built-In "Mechanics Blocks" (3–5 min inside each session)

- **Hand-strike accuracy (Tue/Thu):** 6×3–5 reps med-ball chest "punches" at eye-level from pass-set posture. Emphasize independent hands and rapid recoil. (MB throws are a well-supported tool for upper-body power/RFD.)

- **Anchor posture (Tue):** Isometric half-squat holds at your pass-pro knee and hip angles (angle-specific strength).

- **Base movement (Wed):** Band-resisted lateral shuffles focusing on feet under hips, short steps—keeps your "U-shaped" base under load (transfer with yoke/farmer's gait).

## Accessory Menu (Rotate as needed; keep total sets high)

- **Posterior chain:** GHR, hip hinge variations (RDLs), split-squat patterns
- **Upper back/scap:** chest-supported row, 1-arm cable row, face pulls
- **Triceps/forearm:** close-grip bench, rolling DB extensions; pronation/supination hammer work (for hand fighting)
- **Core (anti-rotation/extension):** Pallof press, dead-bugs, front/side planks with load

Load selection follows NSCA repetition continuum (heavy = strength; moderate = hypertrophy), executed with maximal intent for power.

## Session Structure (Every Day)

1. **Movement prep (8–10 min):** Dynamic hips/ankles/T-spine; light MB throws or skips; 1–2 build-up sprints of 10 m on lower days

2. **Order:** Power → Primary Strength → Secondary Strength/Accessories → OL mechanics block → Conditioning/Finisher. (Power first principle.)

3. **Rest intervals:** Main lifts 2–4 min; power/MB 2–3 min; accessories 60–90 s

4. **Progression rule:** Add load when all prescribed reps move with crisp technique and bar speed doesn't collapse

5. **Hit the Daily Vault work post workout inside the Veldheer lineman Vault.**

## Age & Experience Scaling (Built into the Plan)

14–16/novice track reduces intensities ~10–20% and swaps complex OL variations (e.g., hang/power clean → clean pull) until coached proficiency, consistent with NSCA youth guidance that properly designed, supervised resistance training is safe and effective for youth.

## Optional Testing & KPIs (Track Monthly)

- 10 m sprint
- 5–10–5 shuttle
- Front-squat 3RM
- Bench 3RM
- MB chest throw distance
- Sled push best 10 m time at your max-power load

Use improvements to adjust loads across phases.

- Sled push 10×10 m @ 90% BW

## What This Plan Avoids on Purpose

**Long, steady-state runs.** They don't reflect OL demands and can mute the adaptations we're chasing; NSCA explicitly cautions against distance running for linemen. Your "engine" is better built with repeated high-power efforts that mirror the play clock.
